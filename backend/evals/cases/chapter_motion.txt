Chapter 1: Motion in One Dimension

Displacement is the change in an object's position, measured in a straight line and with a direction. Velocity is the rate of change of position with respect to time. Average velocity equals displacement divided by the elapsed time. Speed is the distance travelled divided by the elapsed time and has no direction.

Students often confuse speed with velocity. Remember that speed has no direction, while velocity does, so an object moving around a circle at constant speed is still changing its velocity.

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity with respect to time. When velocity increases by equal amounts in equal intervals of time, the acceleration is uniform.
=== page ===
Newton's second law of motion states that the net force on an object equals its mass multiplied by its acceleration, written F = ma. The law tells us that a larger net force produces a proportionally larger acceleration, and that for the same force a more massive object accelerates less.

The law holds in an inertial reference frame and for objects whose mass is constant. It does not apply directly to a rocket burning fuel unless the changing mass is accounted for.

Applying the law to a falling object with negligible air resistance gives a constant acceleration g of about 9.8 metres per second squared. Engineers apply the same law when they plan rocket launches and calculate braking distances for trains.

Worked Example 1. A cart accelerates uniformly from rest to 12 m/s in 4 s. Find its acceleration.
Solution. Acceleration is the change in velocity divided by the time taken, so a = (12 - 0) / 4. The acceleration of the cart is 3 m/s^2.
